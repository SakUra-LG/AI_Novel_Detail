The Twenty Bamboo Slips of Mountains and Seas: General Preface

At the Jade Pool on Mount Kunlun, there is a kind of green bamboo slip that records no mortal reign or calendar. It preserves only those moments between heaven and earth that must never be forgotten.

Aman is the keeper of these slips. His full title is Junior Chronicler of Sights and Stories at the Jade Pool, though “Junior Chronicler,” “the Jade Pool chronicler,” and “the young historian” all refer to the same person. The Queen Mother of the West gave him an old brush forever shedding its bristles, a small cloth pouch for his bamboo slips, and a blue-green ribbon embroidered with the rippling pattern of the Jade Pool. She sent him out among the mountains and seas to witness twenty myths with his own eyes and set down an honest account of each.

Aman is not bound by the dynasties of the human world. Whenever a blank green slip begins to glow, it draws him to a moment when a myth is taking place. One instant he may find himself on the ancient riverbank where Nüwa is creating humankind; another, on the scorched earth beneath Hou Yi's bow. In the blink of an eye, he might arrive at Chentang Pass, at the foot of the Great Wall, or inside the academy where Liang Shanbo and Zhu Yingtai study together. When the events have run their course, the blue-green ribbon leads him back to the Jade Pool. The order of his travels, therefore, follows neither human history nor the ages of the gods. It is the “mythic order” in which the bamboo slips choose to open before him.

So when Aman says “last time,” “later,” or “the next slip,” he is describing the stages of his own journey, not claiming that one myth occurred earlier or later than another. The slip called “The Queen Mother of the West” tells how he first received his commission, but its place in this collection does not mean that the commission must have preceded every other tale.

He can cross from age to age, but he cannot see how a story will end. He can stand where a myth unfolds, but he cannot take up its characters' destinies for them. Nüwa must still smelt the stones that mend the sky; Hou Yi must still draw the bow that brings down the suns; the Foolish Old Man and his descendants must still swing their hoes without ceasing if the mountains are to be moved. Aman can do little more than guard his green slips, offer an occasional joke when everyone needs a breath, and—when the truly grave moments come—fall silent and record each person's choice and the price it carries.

Together, the twenty accounts form *The Twenty Bamboo Slips of Mountains and Seas*. The stories do not line up by dynasty. Instead, they catch and reflect one another across a single mythic world: one stone may call to mind both Jingwei and the Foolish Old Man; two far-off shafts of clear light may connect Chang'e and the Weaver Girl; and Aman's increasingly bald old brush follows the guidance of the green slips from one age to the next.

When you open the next slip, remember only this: myths follow an order of their own, and Aman follows a path of his own.
