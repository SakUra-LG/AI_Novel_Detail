Ao Guang's throat rolled out a thunderous roar, his whiskers quivering, the East Sea direction suddenly surged with grayish-white waves, pushing forth a stenchy water vapor that hit them face-on. Nezha took a step back, his pants leg scraping against the threshold, pulling off a button. Aman chased after a few steps, a bamboo slip tucked under his arm, writing "Sea Water Enters the City" as he ran, accidentally writing "Enter" like "Chase". Old Zhao glanced over: "This water is indeed chasing us!" Aman hastily corrected it, still stubborn: "It's chasing itself, my brush can't get flustered." Just as he finished speaking, he stepped into a shallow pit, water splashing up to his chin. He first held the green bamboo slip above his head, then swayed in the water, finally steadying himself; when he looked up, Nezha had already leaped onto the city wall, standing firm against the dragon's shadow in the sky.

The city wall began to rise with water. First, the well edge was flooded, then the rice shop's stairs were submerged, and finally, even the stone lion's foot at the Earth God Temple was no longer visible. Children huddled under the eaves, beating the drums wildly. Nezha untied the Huntian Ling, throwing it fiercely onto the water's surface, the Huntian Ling slicing through the mist, but the next wave still piled on top of the previous one. Aman crouched on the city wall, measuring the water level, ducking his head as the wave came, then peeking out as it receded. Old Zhao asked him if he could see, and he wiped his face, saying: "I can see, the sea is growing faster than my courage." He then quickly added a stroke: Nezha alone tried to block the water, but couldn't.

Nezha refused to admit defeat, spreading the Huntian Ling across the street, tying each end to stone pillars, trying to block the waves. The first wave hit, the Huntian Ling stretched taut, and the crowd was about to cheer, but the second wave came from the sides, slipping through the doorways, pushing a wooden tub, a gourd dipper, and an empty shoe through the streets. Old Zhao chased after his tofu tub, taking several steps, but couldn't catch up, so he shouted to it: "If you want to go to the East Sea, leave tomorrow's tofu money behind!"

Nezha pulled back a segment of the Huntian Ling to rescue the tub, but this let the wave from the left side flood over another level of stairs. He finally realized that the sea water wasn't a stationary opponent to be beaten. It would go around the doors, slip through the alleys, and seep through the cracks in the walls; he blocked one place, but another would cry out for help. Several residents brought wooden boards and sandbags to try to build another barrier in the narrow street. Nezha bent down to help, just as he was pressing down the heaviest sandbag, the wooden boards behind it were pushed askew, and with a loud crack, he hit Aman's face with a wave.

Aman poked his head out from behind the plank, his hair sticking up in three clumps. “I suggest that future history books use characters that can float on water.” Nezha, holding the plank, asked, “What kind of character can float?” Aman wiped the water in front of him. “I don’t know, so I’m only giving advice, not actually making it happen.” Old Zhao slammed a reclaimed wooden basin onto his head and said, “Then use this to float first.” In the heat of the moment, everyone still laughed at the sight, but they all pressed the plank together again to steady it.

The water‑barrier wall held up only three waves before it began to loosen. Nezha saw that an old man was still inside a house and immediately let go of the Huntian Ling on the stone pillar, using a Huntian Ling to glide over the water, gently lifting the old man and his seat to higher ground. With the Huntian Ling no longer holding the waves, the tide rushed into the alley, and the newly built wall immediately fell apart. When Nezha returned to the city wall, he didn’t try the same trick again right away. He stared at the streets that were all in crisis, his chest heaving, and finally understood that fighting the waves at the city’s edge could save one spot but couldn’t protect all of Chen‑Tang‑Guan.

Li Jing immediately ordered the city defenders to report the water level in each street. Mrs. Yin led women and elders to the tallest storage tower, and the townsfolk tied neighboring roofs together with ropes. No one could defeat Ao Guang for Nezha, nor could anyone shoulder the calamity he’d caused; but they first pulled living people out of the water, letting him see that he was facing not a win or loss, but the safety of an entire city. Nezha took back the Huntian Ling, walked along the wall in segments, and prepared to try a different way to stop the flood.

This time he remembered every street that was submerged and every face waiting for his decision.

Nezha descended from the wall and walked down the streets to gauge the water. The salt shop’s burlap sacks were turning white, the shopkeeper and his assistant were carrying the last few bags up onto the rafters; the tofu shop’s stone mill was half exposed, and Old Zhao was still scooping drifting tofu back into a basin with a wooden spoon; at Li family’s courtyard, the wind‑chime grass had sunk into the mud, and the morning bell that had once rang was silent. Nezha reached out to help a woman carrying a child, but she handed the child to her husband and turned to pull her elderly mother. No one had time to blame him or to console him. Aman followed behind, opening his mouth a few times to say something light, but when he saw the bowls and doors lifted by the water, he swallowed his words. He only recorded which families had lost grain, which wall was about to collapse, and which high ground could still hold people. Nezha asked, “What’s the point of keeping all this?” Aman replied, “So future people will know that what you’re carrying isn’t just a dragon king’s curse, but the lives of these people.” Nezha looked at the muddy streets and finally stopped saying, “I can stop it myself.”

Mrs. Yin waded through the water up the wall, gripping Nezha’s wrist. “We’ll think of a solution together for the trouble you caused, but you can’t treat one life as a simple apology.” Nezha looked at her, then at the townsfolk surrounded by water. Li Jing stood a few steps away, his voice hoarse: “If you only show off strength, you’re still a child; if you truly want to take responsibility, first see what you’re actually carrying.” Nezha stayed silent for a long time, then gently pushed his parents’ hands away. He explained to Ao Guang that Li Gen and Ao Bing had both died at his hands, and he didn’t want his parents or the people of Chen‑Tang‑Guan to bear the dragon king’s wrath. He voluntarily took responsibility and offered his life as atonement. Aman kept his old pen steady, didn’t crack a joke, and simply wrote that this choice was spoken and made by Nezha himself.

Nezha turned back to look at the courtyard gate, the salt field, and the people crowded on the high ground, then bowed deeply to his parents. After that, he paid for his sins with his own life. No one saw the gruesome scene. Everyone only saw the Huntian Ling slowly fall from his wrist, the sky‑circle ring clink softly on the green stone, and then silence settled all around. Mrs. Yin rushed forward and hugged him, Li Jing knelt in the water and didn’t get up for a long time. Ao Guang temporarily stopped the water, and Chen‑Tang‑Guan got a breath of relief. Aman still didn’t speak; this page was only wet ink, no jokes.

The Immortal Tai Yi arrived at Chen‑Tang‑Guan and contained Nezha’s soul that had not yet dispersed. He brought lotus flowers and lotus roots, dressing Nezha in petals and shaping him with the roots, slowly reconstructing his form. Li Jing and Mrs. Yin stayed nearby, waiting from dusk to dawn, neither urging nor leaving. The lotus petals gradually closed and then opened again; Nezha finally opened his eyes. He sat up, first looking at his own hands, then at his parents, and softly called out, “Dad, Mom.” Mrs. Yin covered her mouth, Li Jing turned his face away, but his shoulders visibly relaxed. Tai Yi simply said, “Your body has been reshaped; how you use it from now on is still up to you.”

Nezha took a step, his left foot landing solidly, his right knee stiff as he lifted it, his calf muscles tight, nearly tripping over his own shadow. He stood still, rubbed his ankle, and looked up, asking, “Does this body… leak rain?”

Aman was perched on a stone by the riverbank, scribbling away. When he heard the comment, his hand twitched and his old brush left a black dot right next to the “Lotus-Root Incarnation.” He looked up at the sky and said, “I don’t know if it’s leaking rain or not, but if you keep using the Huntian Ling to bathe, the Chentang Pass will definitely start leaking.” Nezha tried to take a couple of steps, but his right foot went wonky and almost caught the hem of his own robe. He straightened up at once, “Just grew these legs, still learning the way.” Aman nodded seriously, “I’ll write that down so you don’t blame the ground when you fall again.” Nezha plucked a lotus petal and stuck it on Aman’s forehead, “Then you wear one too, so the old brush doesn’t lose its hair to the wind.” They gave each other a quick stare, and Lady Yin finally burst out laughing.

Nezha’s steps were like a frog just out of a mud pit—wobbly and unsteady, his toe tips constantly snagging on the edge of his robe, as if an invisible hand were gently nudging him. Aman’s brush tip danced across the bamboo slip, trembling a bit whenever Nezha’s slight wobble made the page shake, so the characters slanted like an impromptu poem. Nezha lifted his head, squinting at the distant sky as if searching for a path that would steady his footing. Aman rested his pen lightly on his shoulder, as if giving himself a little boost, muttering, “Don’t let that path turn into a slide.” Nezha grinned, then pressed the lotus petal onto his own forehead, crowning himself with a tiny flower—playful and cute.

On Aman’s green bamboo slip, besides the black dot beside “Lotus-Root Incarnation,” a few lines read, “Nezha’s steps are unsteady; more practice needed.” Lady Yin stood nearby, cradling a bowl of cool tea, glancing up at Nezha now and then with a mix of worry and a hint of relief. Nezha gently shook the Huntian Ling onto the ground, turning his little “bath” routine into a theatrical performance. Aman glanced at the sky and whispered, “If you keep using the Huntian Ling as swimwear, the water at Chentang Pass will rise with you.” Nezha laughed, as if saying, “Then let it rise a bit—surprise everyone!” Lady Yin couldn’t help but laugh, and the whole riverside instantly felt light and cheerful.

When the East Sea surged again, Nezha marched out of the city with the Lotus-Root Incarnation in tow. Ao Guang transformed into a black dragon coiling over the waves, still demanding that Chentang Pass pay for Ao Bing’s life. Nezha didn’t retreat, nor did he let Taiyi Zhenren or his parents step forward. He flung the Huntian Ling, wrapping the dragon’s horns in crimson ribbons; Ao Guang thrashed his tail, raising a wall of water, and Nezha leapt on the crest, striking the dragon’s claws with the Qiankun Ring. The magical tools were the same old ones, but their wielder no longer fought just for a quick thrill. Whenever a massive wave barreled toward the villagers, Nezha first split the surf to shield the shore, then turned to face Ao Guang. After a back‑and‑forth, Ao Guang became bound by the Huntian Ling, the Qiankun Ring hanging before him, unable to flood the city any longer.

Nezha didn’t take his life; he said, “I’ve already repaid Li Gen and Ao Bing with my own life. If you still want to vent on the people, I’ll block you again.” Seeing the villagers retreat to higher ground and spotting Li Jing and Lady Yin behind Nezha, Ao Guang finally cooled his anger. He conceded and withdrew the water. The waves receded layer by layer back into the East Sea, and the roofs, fields, and docks of Chentang Pass reappeared. Nezha personally subdued the Dragon King and halted the flood, but he didn’t brag about the victory—he simply turned to help right the door panels that the water had knocked askew.

After the water went down, Nezha didn’t rush home. A gate panel on East Street was jammed in a stone ditch, so he and a few carpenters lifted it out; the salt‑field drainage ditch was clogged with seaweed, and he rolled up his pant legs to clear it until dusk darkened the sky; a small fishing boat sat stuck on the dock steps, and ten people couldn’t budge it. Nezha tied the Huntian Ling to the boat’s bow, but first asked the old boatman how to tie the knot. The boatman, surprised that Nezha actually asked, deliberately slowed down, “First call me ‘Master.’” Nezha stared, “My master would swap bodies with lotus roots—can you do that?” The boatman patted the hull, “I only know how to keep a boat from falling apart, and today you need that.” Nezha thought a moment and finally called out, “Old master!” Everyone laughed, and together they pushed the boat back into the water. Aman recorded everything, and when he wrote “Nezha listened to advice,” he stopped his brush and read it three times, fearing the sea might have given him a hallucination. Nezha snatched the slip, “Write those three characters bigger.” Aman protected the green bamboo slip, “Rare things should be written small, so they don’t fill the whole page and leave no room to note the next mishap you cause.” Nezha stubbornly claimed he wouldn’t cause any more trouble, but the Huntian Ling snagged an empty bucket at his feet, clanged loudly, and the whole street heard the crash.

The wind returned, carrying the scent of mud and green duckweed, drying the blue printed cloth on the clothesline and blowing open the uncovered vat of new soy sauce at the entrance of the sauce factory. The aroma wafted up, sweet, spicy, and savory, filling the entire street. The water receded quickly, and before the salt grains on the drying field had a chance to fully absorb the moisture, children were already crouched by the puddles, scooping up tadpoles. Aman sat on the threshold of the City God Temple, mending the last piece of green bamboo slip. The cool moss seeped through his pants, creeping up his legs. He had been licking his brush for too long, and his tongue felt numb. As he wrote the characters "stop the water disaster," his hand trembled, and the character "stop" was missing a stroke, becoming "correct" instead. He stared at it for a moment, deciding not to change it, and instead used his knife to carve a small hook beside it: "This 'correct' is not the same as that 'correct,' but rather means the water returning to its place."

Nezha crouched beside him, holding a bowl of hot ginger soup. Madame Yin stood under the old pagoda tree, mending an umbrella. She glanced up at her son, then bent her head to thread her needle, as if sewing one more seam could reattach the broken days. Li Jing removed his armor and washed the mud off his mirror at the well. He scooped up a ladle of clear water and splashed it on his face. As he opened his eyes, he saw Nezha approaching with an empty bowl, and silently took it from him, placing it on the well's pulley. The father and son didn't speak, only the sound of the well rope creaking filled the air. The Huntian Ling was draped over the courtyard fence, dripping water, and the red silk absorbed the sunlight, warming the window paper. Old Zhao from the tofu shop next door lifted the curtain and poked his head out: "Young master, would you like some vegetarian dumplings today? Scallions and eggs, plenty to go around!" Nezha grinned: "I'll take double the vinegar!"

Aman dried the last green bamboo slip and bound it with a wisteria vine, adding it to "The Twenty Bamboo Slips of Mountains and Seas." He wrote about the morning at Chentang Pass: Nezha using the Huntian Ling to stir up the Eastern Sea, the Yaksha Li Gen asking for punishment, the Dragon Prince Ao Bing following to fight, and the Eastern Sea Dragon King Ao Guang unleashing a flood. Nezha took responsibility and apologized with his death, and the True Person of Taiyi used a lotus flower and lotus root to bring him back to life. After his rebirth, Nezha used the Huntian Ling and the Heaven and Earth Circle to subdue the Dragon King and stop the flood. As he wrote this, he thought of the bamboo slip about the Eight Immortals crossing the sea and whispered: "The Eight Immortals treated the sea as a road, while Nezha first brought the sea's temper ashore, and in the end, he also shouldered his own responsibility."

Nezha peeked at the slip: "Finished?" Aman quickly hugged the green bamboo slip tight: "Finished, can't get it wet anymore." Nezha pointed to his still-dripping sleeve: "The sea didn't follow, it's you who didn't wring it out." Aman looked down, feeling embarrassed, and twisted his sleeve to squeeze out a small stream of water. He still refused to admit defeat: "This is called leaving some on-site evidence." Li Jing chimed in: "The more evidence you have, the more you'll sleep in the fish basket tonight." Everyone laughed for a while. After the laughter subsided, Aman deepened the strokes of the characters "Nezha taking responsibility." This time, his old brush didn't write crookedly.
